Heater Calibration

About These Values

Calibrate these three hardware values once. They describe the heater and element, not a particular brew profile.

Heater Power

Enter the actual electrical heater power in watts. Use the rated power of the element, or measure it at the operating voltage.

Heat Transfer Coefficient (k)

This describes how quickly heat moves from the element into the water, in watts per degree Celsius. Use plain water and the same mixer and hardware arrangement used during brewing.

Element Thermal Mass (C_e)

This describes how much heat the element can store per degree Celsius, in joules per degree Celsius. It is estimated together with k from heating behavior and should remain stable for the same hardware.

Prepare the Test
  1. Add the selected volume of plain water to the bowl.
  2. Put the sensor in the mixed water, away from the heater, and start the mixer.
  3. In Settings, enter the heater's measured or rated Heater Power. The calibration page reads that value automatically.
  4. Keep the same vessel, sensor position, mixer speed, and heater voltage used for brewing.

How the Result Is Calculated

The device logs temperature every minute. The page uses every logged value to simulate the two thermal masses and searches for the `k` and `C_e` pair with the smallest curve error.

C_w = water volume × 4186 J/(kg·°C)
Q_water = C_w × (T_heat-end − T0)
P_water = Q_water / heating time
τ_element = C_e / k
Fit error = sum(model temperature − logged temperature)²

The test stops at 50 °C and after a 2 °C measured cooldown drop. It does not wait for the water to reach ambient temperature. Do not add grain during calibration, use the same heater power and mixer arrangement as the real brew, and never leave the heater running unattended.
