Heater Calibration

What Is Measured

The element and pot base store heat while the heater is on. After switch-off that heat keeps flowing into the water, causing temperature overshoot. Because water viscosity drops and kettle convection speeds up as water warms, heating dynamics and heat loss change significantly between low rests (e.g. 50 °C) and high rests (e.g. 70 °C).

Dual-Anchor Calibration (50 °C & 70 °C)

The automated test measures thermal parameters at two fixed anchors: 50 °C (low mash rests) and 70 °C (saccharification / mash-out). During brewing, the controller dynamically interpolates between these anchors to guarantee dead-on accuracy across your entire mash schedule.

Time Constant (τ)

How quickly stored heat transfers from element to the temperature sensor, in seconds. As water warms and thins, convection speeds up, so τ decreases.

Effective Power

The true heating power that reaches the water, measured from the heating rate.

Store Gain

Ratio of total measured rise to pure element thermal capacity. It accounts for pot base retention and sensor response characteristics.

Dynamic Heat Loss

The kettle's heat loss to the room in watts, fit to a non-linear power law curve between 50 °C and 70 °C.

Prepare the Test
  1. Fill the pot with the volume of plain water you normally mash with. Cold tap water (15–20 °C) is ideal.
  2. Keep the sensor, lid, mixer and mixer cycle exactly as during brewing. The mixer runs its normal cycle during the test.
  3. In Settings, enter the heater's rated Heater Power so the page can compare it with the measured value.
  4. The test runs automatically in two sequential stages: it heats to 50 °C, detects the peak and confirmed cooling drop, then heats to 70 °C and waits for the final peak drop.

How the Result Is Calculated

The sensor is read every 2 seconds and the readings are averaged over each full mixer cycle, matching the controller's brewing filter. The test heats to 50 °C, turns off the heater, and watches for the overshoot peak and a confirmed drop. It then immediately switches back on to ramp to 70 °C and detects the second peak and drop.

Coast fit: T(t) = T_off + ΔT·(1 − e−t/τ) − r·t
C_w = water volume × 4186 J/°C
Loss P(T) = P_1 · (ΔT / ΔT_1)α
τ(T) = τ_1 + (τ_2 − τ_1) · (T − 50) / 20
Store gain(T) = S_1 + (S_2 − S_1) · (T − 50) / 20
