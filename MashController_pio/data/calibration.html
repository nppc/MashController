Heater Calibration

What Is Measured

The element and pot base store heat while the heater is on. After switch-off that heat keeps flowing into the water, so the temperature keeps rising. A short pulse stores little heat; a long heat stores the most. The controller tracks this stored heat and switches off early by the right amount.

Time Constant (τ)

How quickly stored heat reaches the sensor, in seconds. It combines the element, the pot base, mixing and the sensor itself. After a long heat the stored energy is about effective power × τ.

Effective Power

The power that actually reaches the water, measured from the heating rate. It is usually a little below the rated power.

Store Gain

The measured overshoot divided by effective power × τ. It is usually a little above 1, because sensor lag and mixing make the overshoot look larger than the element's stored heat alone. The controller scales its prediction by it.

Heat Loss

How fast the pot loses heat to the room, in watts per °C above room temperature. This is what the heater has to make up during a rest.

Prepare the Test
  1. Fill the pot with the volume of plain water you normally mash with, at least 15 °C below the target. Cold tap water is best.
  2. Keep the sensor, lid, mixer and mixer cycle exactly as during brewing. The mixer runs its normal cycle during the test.
  3. In Settings, enter the heater's rated Heater Power so the page can compare it with the measured value.
  4. Set the target to your usual rest temperature. For 20 L the test takes roughly 45-75 minutes.

How the Result Is Calculated

The sensor is read every 2 seconds and the readings are averaged over each full mixer cycle (mixing plus rest), which cancels the ripple the mixer causes. The controller averages the same way during brewing. The heater runs at full power until the target is reached, then switches off. Logging continues until the peak has passed and the water has started to cool.

Coast fit: T(t) = T_off + ΔT·(1 − e−t/τ) − r·t
C_w = water volume × 4186 J/°C
Heat loss = r × C_w / (T_water − T_room)
Effective power = C_w × heating rate + heat loss
Store gain = ΔT × C_w / (effective power × τ)

The page warns when the result is uncertain, for example when the heating was too short, the peak had not clearly passed, or the two power estimates disagree. Do not add grain during calibration and never leave the heater running unattended.
